# Geode visual references: native spatial additions and honest controls

Research and screenshot review: **7 October 2026**. Status: design evidence, not an implemented or benchmarked renderer.

## Direction fixed by the owner

Preserve **every existing C++ visual style and MilkDrop/projectM**. Add psychedelic 3D tunnels, sculptural objects, and music-driven camera movement. All new simulation, scene generation, camera direction, and rendering belong in the **native C++ engine with GPU shaders**. JavaScript examples are research references only: no WebView, JavaScript renderer, or JavaScript runtime in the app. Kotlin continues to provide the Android shell and platform integrations such as Media3, authentication, and billing; Oboe remains part of the native audio architecture.

The default experience is **fresh, generative, continuous live motion**, influenced by music and touch. Do not ship a repeated seeded camera path or a short repeated animation as the default. Bounded stochastic evolution should produce coherent movement, not independent random jitter each frame. Record the actual rendered performance with its audio; starting the same song again must not be required to reconstruct what the user recorded. Fixed random sources and clocks are permitted in test harnesses only. This supersedes older blueprint references to a default seeded spline.

## Evidence boundaries

- **O — observed:** a still image actually inspected, either an official store gallery image or the user's supplied screenshot. This supports composition, visible UI, color, and apparent depth only.
- **D — documented:** a publisher's public description or help document. This establishes the advertised behavior, not its measured quality.
- **P — proposed:** a design transfer for Geode. This is our interpretation, not a claim about another app's internals.

No competing app was installed or exercised. Official trailer links were identified, but playable video review was unavailable; there are no claims here about measured smoothness, camera trajectories, frame rate, audio latency, or whether a competitor uses meshes, ray marching, feedback, or prerecorded media. A tunnel-looking still is not proof of a true 3D camera. A phone tilted in marketing artwork is not an in-app camera effect.

## Ranked design lessons

1. **Build space before decorating it.** The strongest tunnel references communicate a near layer, a receding structure, and a destination. New spatial scenes need perspective, parallax, occlusion, consistent scale, and a stable focal region. Screen rotation, UV zoom, fog, and bloom alone do not deliver those properties. Existing planar effects remain valuable and should retain their identities.
2. **Let music and touch shape an evolving journey.** Use continuing native state with fresh session variation, smoothly changing local goals, and bounded acceleration. Separate slow structural evolution from short musical accents. Bass can open a passage or widen a shape; midrange can change curl or articulation; high frequencies can produce sparse highlights. Confidence-weighted musical phrase cues can influence route choices. Do not jerk the whole camera on every onset, restart a fixed path on every track, or replace musical response with uncorrelated random motion.
3. **Give every scene a recognizable silhouette and material.** Mineral architecture, glass ribbons, pigment sheets, fibers, petals, and terrain should differ in shape, lighting, and motion at neutral color settings. Preserve dark space and midtones. A restrained bloom setting must still look finished. “Psychedelic” can mean surprising structure and fluid transformation; it does not require constant full-screen saturation or flashing.
4. **Separate camera, object motion, and audio response.** A user must be able to hold the view while forms react to music, or move through a quiet scene. Touch orientation is a pose change; automatic orbit is a rate/behavior. Camera motion zero should hold automatic travel; response zero should stop audio deformation. Scene-specific ambient evolution is a separately named control. Exact implementation is owned by the native camera design.
5. **Make foreground, atmosphere, and palette independent where meaningful.** The Astral selector is useful evidence for this separation. Geode can offer authored background/atmosphere choices without multiplying every combination into a separate scene. A dark background must remain a valid deliberate option.
6. **Make live adjustments immediately trustworthy.** A few consequential controls with visible results are better than a universal wall of sliders. Show spatial controls only for implemented spatial adapters. Presets retain the complete visual state; restoring a preset must restore the same configuration while starting a fresh live evolution, unless replaying a recorded performance.
7. **Treat recording as capture of a performance.** The exported result must preserve the actual music, camera movement, touch, transitions, and visible scene from the session. A later render of a freshly randomized scene is not the same recording. Offline rerender, if added, requires an explicit captured timeline/state contract and must be described separately.

## Current Customize controls: naming and behavior corrections required

Read-only source inspection found the following. This report makes no app changes. The exact native camera architecture is documented by the engine workstream; these are UI acceptance requirements.

| Current path/control | Current evidence | Required user-facing contract |
|---|---|---|
| `CustomizeTabs.kt` Rotation → `SceneParams.rotation` | `ShaderScene.cpp` and `MilkdropScene.cpp` integrate this value over time; it is an angular rate. `TouchTransform.rotation` adds gesture degrees multiplied by `0.012` into that same rate. | Separate **Angle / orientation** from **Spin speed**. A twist must not silently increase indefinite spin. Preserve old presets through explicit migration rather than reinterpreting their stored values. |
| Zoom / Endless zoom | Current shared framing uses UV scaling; the existing zoom phase is cyclic. It cannot guarantee spatial forward travel. | **Scale** or **Framing zoom** for planar output. **Travel / Dolly** only for a spatial adapter. Retain legacy endless-zoom looks with accurate labeling and migrate new spatial camera state separately. |
| Sway / Drift X/Y | Existing helpers use time-driven oscillation or UV displacement; they are not a general world-space translation. | Preserve existing visual behavior, but describe it as planar movement. New **Camera drift** must change camera pose and keep continuity; do not map it to the old slider merely because names sound similar. |
| `motionOrbit` | Field is serialized and crosses the parameter boundary, but the audit did not find a functioning universal orbit consumer. | Do not advertise a working orbit control until a scene implements it and preview/capture both consume it. |
| Speed / motion amount | Animation time, motion modulation, and scene-local behavior are currently mixed across families. | Separate **Scene evolution**, **Camera motion**, and **Audio response**. Preserve old style values and expose only meaningful controls for that family's capabilities. |
| Pulse / shake / beat response | Current native inspection includes hardcoded zero beat response in the shader path; separate composite pulse/shake behavior is not established by fixing that uniform alone. | Require a visible end-to-end result before enabling an advertised response control. Audio freshness and envelope corrections are a separate engine task. Do not mark the full response system fixed by a UI rename. |

## Remaining visual and motion verification

Official demo leads, **not watched in this review**: [Astral store trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vne2nEOQ3gA), [Vythm store trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=da4ZiMxpUFE), [Avee store trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jNL8SHighDM), [Magic Fluids developer-linked demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FdhRi6Zsh5w), and [Mobile Visuals developer channel](https://www.youtube.com/user/EdgarVarningsson/videos). A later review should record actual timecodes and distinguish camera translation, object deformation, and screen-space warp. Do not backfill this document with invented video observations.

For Geode, the first reusable native spatial slice should demonstrate one corridor and one object scene before multiplying variants. Acceptance evidence must include:

1. Short portrait and landscape clips with a recognizable near/mid/far structure, occlusion, and lateral parallax. Show camera held while the object evolves, then object held while camera moves.
2. Silence, a steady beat, varied music, touch steering, fast touch release, and recenter. Show separate zero states for camera movement and audio response. No abrupt velocity jump, invalid pose, or teleport on source/preset changes.
3. Two fresh live sessions with the same preset and music that produce different evolving trajectories; inspect a longer continuous session for obvious short loops. This demonstrates fresh initialization and variation, not a mathematical guarantee of never repeating any shape.
4. Save/load of every new control; exact legacy preset retention; the same camera/material state in preview and captured video. Validate that a recorded touch gesture and scene transition remain in the resulting recording rather than being regenerated afterward.
5. Real-device GPU/CPU, thermal, memory, sustained frame pacing, and recording results on the declared device tiers. Reduce detail gracefully before changing scene identity. Still images and a successful APK build alone cannot establish high-end motion quality.
6. A retained-style regression catalog: all existing C++ scene IDs and MilkDrop remain selectable, render correctly, preserve saved values, and record. Adding the six scenes must not narrow the user's existing catalog.

No local build, test, lint, benchmark, or app mutation was performed for this report. CI and device evidence remain release gates, not completed results.
